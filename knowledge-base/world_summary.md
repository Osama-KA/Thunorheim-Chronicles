# Thunorheim — World Summary

## Overview
Thunorheim is a continent of competing human kingdoms, old noble houses, 
and frontier settlements bound together by one existential threat: the Blight.
The Blight is a spreading corruption that seeps from deep within the earth,
warping creatures into monsters and rotting the land it touches. Regions 
consumed by the Blight are called the Greymark. Nobody knows where it 
started. The Greymark grows slowly but it never retreats.

## tone
Thunorheim carries the weight of a world that has been fighting a losing 
battle for generations. It is not a world of grand heroic destiny. It is a 
world of hard choices, tired soldiers, and frontier towns that celebrate 
survival as victory. The aesthetic draws from Norse tradition — runestones 
line forgotten roads, burial mounds dot the hillsides, and sagas are sung 
in taverns about those who held the line when the Greymark surged. But the 
bones are grounded high fantasy. Magic exists but it costs something. 
Healing works but it is controversial. Nothing is free.

## The Blight
The Blight is not a force with a face or a will anyone has confirmed. It 
seeps upward through the earth in regions called Blightseams — cracks in 
the ground that weep a dark viscous fluid locals call greywater. Creatures 
that drink greywater or spend too long near a Blightseam begin to change. 
Their eyes cloud over. Their behavior becomes erratic. Within days or weeks 
they become something else entirely — faster, more aggressive, structurally 
wrong in ways that are hard to describe and easy to recognize.

Land touched by the Blight does not recover. Crops fail. Water goes foul. 
The soil turns grey and hardens into a brittle crust that crumbles underfoot.
What the Blight touches it keeps.

## Riftings
Riftings are unpredictable surges of corrupted creatures from deep within 
the Greymark. They happen without warning, last between one and three days, 
and devastate any settlement caught unprepared. Nobody has successfully 
predicted a Rifting. The best anyone has managed is early detection — 
Wardens posted near the Greymark edge who can send riders ahead of the wave.

Riftings are increasing in frequency. Three years ago they came twice a year.
Last year there were seven. Nobody in power will say this out loud.

## The Warden's Guild
The Warden's Guild is the only institution in Thunorheim that crosses 
kingdom borders without political friction. Founded two centuries ago after 
a catastrophic Rifting destroyed the city of Valdengard, the Guild exists 
for one purpose: put trained operatives between the Greymark and the people.

Wardens are licensed hunters and scouts. They take contracts, investigate 
Blightseams, track corrupted creatures, and send intelligence back to Guild 
headquarters in the frontier city of Greyhold. They are not soldiers and 
they are not heroes. They are professionals.

The Guild is currently under pressure from two directions. Frontier 
settlements are demanding more Wardens than the Guild can supply. And 
several kingdoms have begun quietly withdrawing financial support, 
preferring to spend on their own armies rather than a cross-border 
institution they cannot fully control.

## Magic — Runecraft
Magic in Thunorheim is not cast. It is inscribed. Runescribes carve 
temporary or permanent runes onto surfaces, objects, and their own bodies 
to produce specific effects. A rune of heat on a blade makes it burn. A 
rune of silence on a door makes it soundless. A rune of warding on skin 
deflects a blow.

Runecraft requires preparation, materials, and knowledge. You cannot 
improvise a rune under pressure unless you have practiced it hundreds of 
times. The tradition draws from the old saga runes — an ancient system 
developed by the first settlers of Thunorheim who carved meaning into 
everything they built.

## Thorncraft
Thorncraft is a frontier healing tradition that uses Blight-adjacent herbs,
corrupted materials, and controlled exposure to greywater derivatives to 
accelerate healing and neutralize corruption. It works. It works better 
than anything else available on the frontier.

It is also deeply controversial. The kingdoms view Thorncraft as dangerous 
border sorcery. The church considers it heretical. Frontier towns do not 
care — when a Warden comes back from the Greymark half-corrupted, a 
Thornwarden is the only thing that saves them.

Thornwardens walk a permanent line between curing corruption and wielding 
it. Some have crossed that line. The ones who crossed it and came back are 
the most effective healers in the world. The ones who did not come back 
are part of what Wardens get sent to deal with.

## The Central Conflict
Something in the Greymark is changing. Riftings are accelerating. 
Blightseams are opening in regions that were stable for decades. Three 
Warden outposts have gone silent in the last six months. The Guild's 
senior investigators believe something is actively destabilizing the 
Greymark from within — not a natural progression but a directed one.

The kingdoms deny there is a problem. The Guild knows there is. And 
somewhere in the Greymark, past the last outpost and beyond the edge of 
mapped territory, something is answering questions nobody has asked yet.
