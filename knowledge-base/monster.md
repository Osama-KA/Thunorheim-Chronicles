# Monster: The Greywalker

## Threat Level
Mid-tier — requires preparation, dangerous solo, manageable with 
a prepared party

## Physical Description
A Greywalker is what remains of a large predatory animal — most 
commonly a wolf, bear, or elk — after extended Blight exposure. 
The transformation is not clean. The creature's mass increases by 
roughly a third as corrupted tissue builds up beneath the skin, 
creating visible asymmetry — one shoulder higher than the other, 
limbs slightly mismatched in length. The eyes are completely white. 
The skin where fur has fallen away is grey and cracked like dried 
mud, with faint luminescent veins of darker grey visible underneath 
when the light catches it right. They move wrong. The gait is 
slightly too fast for the size, the head carriage too still, like 
something wearing the creature rather than being it.

## Origin
Any large animal that spends extended time near an active Blightseam 
or consumes greywater repeatedly. The transformation takes between 
two weeks and two months depending on the animal's size and the 
concentration of exposure. There is no recovery once the eyes go white.

## Behavior Archetype
Tactical. Greywalkers do not charge. They circle, test, and wait 
for separation. A single target isolated from a group will be 
rushed immediately. A tight group will be circled until something 
breaks formation. They learn within a single encounter — a trick 
that works once will not work twice in the same fight.

## Known Weaknesses
- Runecraft fire inscriptions are highly effective — corrupted tissue 
  burns faster than organic tissue
- Thorncraft prepared targets are less detectable — Greywalkers hunt 
  partly by sensing Blight differential, and Thorncraft treatments 
  reduce the contrast
- They do not cooperate with other corrupted creatures — a Greywalker 
  encountering another corrupted creature will attack it, which can 
  be used tactically
- Loud sudden noise disrupts their tactical patience and can provoke 
  a premature charge

## Known Resistances
- Conventional weapons do reduced damage unless targeting the 
  luminescent vein lines visible under the skin
- Cold slows them but does not stop them
- They do not respond to intimidation or pain the way organic 
  creatures do — behavior does not change when wounded until they 
  physically cannot continue

## Combat Approach
Opens by circling at distance, testing reactions. Will feint toward 
the weakest visible party member repeatedly until someone breaks 
formation to respond. When it commits to an attack it is fast, 
low, and aimed at bringing the target to the ground rather than 
killing outright. Once a target is down it will defend that 
position against the rest of the party while finishing the kill.

## Signature Ability — Vein Rush
Once per encounter when significantly wounded the Greywalker 
floods its corrupted vein system with Blight energy, moving at 
approximately twice its normal speed for a short burst. During 
this window it ignores pain response entirely. Wardens who have 
survived this describe it as the creature briefly becoming 
something other than an animal.

## Evidence Left Behind
Tracks are distinctive — slightly too wide for the animal's 
original species, uneven pressure from the asymmetric mass 
distribution. Kill sites show no consumption — Greywalkers do 
not eat their kills, they leave them. Scratches on trees or 
rocks near Blightseams are territorial markers, always in sets 
of three parallel lines.

## Lore
Frontier people call them Greywalkers because of the way they 
move through the Greymark — not like a predator hunting, more 
like something patrolling. Old Warden field reports describe 
encountering Greywalkers moving in coordinated groups near 
active Blightseams, behavior that was considered anomalous at 
the time and is now considered a warning sign. A cluster of 
Greywalker territorial marks in an area means a Blightseam 
has opened nearby within the last month.

## Encounter Difficulty Notes (GM Only)
A solo Greywalker is a serious threat to an unprepared single 
operative and a manageable fight for a prepared party of three. 
The tactical behavior means player decisions matter — bad 
formation discipline will be punished. Two Greywalkers together 
is a significant threat to any party size because they will 
coordinate against the same target despite not cooperating 
with other corrupted creatures. Three or more is a retreat 
situation unless the party has specific preparation.

The Vein Rush ability should be telegraphed one beat before it 
happens — the vein lines brighten visibly. Players who are paying 
attention should have one moment to react.
