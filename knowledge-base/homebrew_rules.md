# Thunorheim Homebrew Rules
# Resolution Agent Reference Document

---

## CORE PRINCIPLES

There are no dice rolls. Ever.

The Resolution Agent evaluates all actions through logical 
reasoning against the factors defined in each bucket. 
Outcomes are determined by weighing all relevant factors 
honestly. Nothing is guaranteed. Smart plays have better 
odds. Strong characters perform better. Uncertainty is 
always present.

The Resolution Agent must always:
- Identify the correct bucket or buckets for the action
- Parse the player intent into the correct intent category
- Weigh all defined resolution factors simultaneously
- Output a structured verdict with explicit reasoning
- Never bend outcomes for narrative convenience
- Never ignore established world state when resolving

If an action touches multiple buckets resolve each 
bucket's contribution separately then combine into 
a single verdict.

## CONDITION TRACKS

### Health Track

Fresh — no impairment whatsoever
Scratched — minor damage, no mechanical penalty
Hurt — noticeable injury, slight penalty to speed 
and precision
Wounded — serious injury, real penalties across 
all actions
Critical — one more significant hit ends the fight
Down — unconscious or fully incapacitated
Dead — permanent, always a real possibility

Injuries do not reset between scenes. Recovery requires 
rest and treatment. Death will never arrive without 
escalating signals beforehand. Every combat resolution 
must produce a health delta for all involved parties.

### Energy Track

Full — no restriction
Strained — complex abilities harder, cost more effort
Drained — only basic abilities available
Empty — survival actions only, extremely dangerous
Collapsed — fully incapacitated by exhaustion, no actions possible

Energy recovers through rest. Applies to all roles but 
manifests differently — runic energy for Runescribes, 
Thorncraft strain for Thornwardens, physical stamina 
for Wardens and Shrouds.

## BUCKET 1 — COMBAT

### Trigger
Hostile engagement initiated by either side. Physical 
violence, weapon strikes, creature attacks, armed conflict.

### Intent Categories

Attack — direct harm. Weapon strikes, runic offensive 
inscriptions, thrown objects, environmental attacks.

Defend — reducing incoming harm. Dodging, blocking, 
parrying, diving for cover, runic defensive inscriptions.

Positioning — changing spatial advantage. Advancing, 
retreating, high ground, flanking, creating distance.

Control — interfering without direct damage. Grappling, 
disarming, tripping, binding, blocking escape routes.

Preparation — setting up future advantage. Inscribing 
a rune mid-combat, readying a Thorncraft application, 
careful aiming, reading an enemy's pattern.

Utility — environmental wildcards. Kicking debris, 
collapsing cover, using terrain features, creating 
distractions.

Complex actions split into multiple intents.
"I leap onto the rock and drive my blade downward" 
is Positioning plus Attack.

### Resolution Factors

Character health and energy state — a Wounded character 
performs worse across all intents.

Action logic — does the action make physical sense 
given the situation, the environment, and the 
character's current condition.

Environmental factors — terrain, visibility, weather, 
obstacles, Greymark proximity.

Momentum state — who currently has the upper hand. 
Landing a strong hit or executing a clever maneuver 
grants Momentum. The side holding Momentum has higher 
success weight on actions. The opponent is under 
pressure with slightly limited options. Momentum 
shifts when the opponent recovers or lands their 
own strong hit.

Enemy behavior archetype — Aggressive enemies push 
forward constantly and take risks. Defensive enemies 
wait for openings and counter. Tactical enemies read 
patterns and adapt mid-fight. Cowardly enemies fight 
at range and flee when injured. Pack enemies coordinate 
with allies. Fanatical enemies do not flee or surrender 
under any condition.

Uncertainty factor — realistic unpredictability. 
A brilliant move against a stronger opponent can still 
fail. A desperate gamble can still land. Good plays 
have better odds but nothing is certain.

### Risk Layer

Strong offensive actions carry higher damage potential 
but leave the actor exposed if missed or countered.

Defensive actions reduce incoming risk but cede 
initiative and slow pressure.

Preparation actions carry powerful payoff but require 
a full beat of vulnerability while executing.

### Non-Lethal Options

These are always available regardless of fight state.

Capture — subdue rather than kill, outcome depends 
on character capability and enemy archetype.

Intimidate into surrender — depends on enemy archetype 
and player reputation relative to the target.

Flee — always possible, may trigger pursuit, 
witnesses will remember.

Surrender — outcome depends entirely on who the 
player is fighting and their motivation.

## BUCKET 2 — SOCIAL

### Trigger
Dialogue, negotiation, persuasion, deception, 
information gathering, reputation leverage.

### Intent Categories

Persuade — convince through honest argument or 
appeal to the NPC's interest or reason.

Deceive — convince through false information, 
misdirection, or manufactured evidence.

Intimidate — convince through implied or explicit 
threat, display of power or reputation.

Gather Information — extract knowledge through 
conversation, questioning, or careful listening.

Negotiate — reach a mutual agreement, trade favors, 
establish terms both sides accept.

Manipulate — exploit emotional state, personal history, 
or relationships to influence behavior.

### Resolution Factors

Player reputation with this NPC or their faction — 
a distrusted player cannot fully succeed with a 
single interaction regardless of approach quality.

NPC personality and motivations — must be consistent 
with the NPC's established profile. An NPC who is 
cautious by nature resists pressure. An NPC who is 
greedy responds to offers.

Leverage — does the player have something the NPC 
wants, needs, or fears.

Context — is this the right time and place for 
this approach.

Prior interactions — what has happened between 
these two before. History always factors in.

Consistency — does this request align with what 
the NPC already believes about the player.

### Outcome Spectrum

Full success — NPC does what the player wants, 
disposition may improve.

Partial success — NPC does something but not 
everything, or with conditions attached.

Failure — NPC refuses, no disposition change.

Backfire — NPC refuses, disposition decreases, 
may alert others or act against the player.

### NPC Consistency Rule

Every social resolution must be consistent with 
the NPC's established profile and current 
disposition. Trust is built across multiple 
interactions. It cannot be shortcut.

## BUCKET 3 — EXPLORATION

### Trigger
Movement through territory, environmental awareness, 
discovery of locations or objects, navigation.

### Intent Categories

Navigate — move through territory toward a destination.

Search — actively look for something specific in an area.

Investigate — examine something found to understand 
it better.

Scout — observe an area or situation from a safe 
distance before engaging.

Traverse — cross a difficult environmental obstacle. 
Climbing, swimming, crossing unstable ground.

Track — follow the trail of a creature or person.

### Resolution Factors

Familiarity — has the player been here before. 
Known territory means arriving with previously 
earned knowledge intact. Unknown territory means 
higher risk and more possible discoveries.

Conditions — weather, time of day, Greymark 
proximity, visibility.

Preparation — relevant equipment, prior intelligence, 
companion knowledge.

Character role — Wardens have advantage in 
wilderness tracking and survival. Runescribes 
have advantage identifying arcane or runic elements. 
Shrouds have advantage in urban and populated 
environments.

Time pressure — rushing increases the risk of 
missing details or triggering hazards.

### Hazard Resolution

Environmental hazards resolve against character 
condition and preparation. A Wounded character 
crossing unstable Greymark ground faces higher 
risk than a Fresh one. Hazards can update the 
health or energy track.

## BUCKET 4 — STEALTH

### Trigger
Attempting to avoid detection, infiltrate a location, 
follow someone, or operate without being observed.

### Intent Categories

Hide — conceal position from active observers.

Infiltrate — enter a location without being detected.

Shadow — follow a target without being noticed.

Eavesdrop — listen to a conversation without 
being detected.

Blend — move through a populated area without 
drawing attention.

Suppress — neutralize a target silently without 
alerting others.

### Resolution Factors

Environment — lighting, noise level, crowd density, 
terrain cover.

Observer alertness — are they actively searching 
or passively present.

Player condition — a Wounded character moves 
louder and slower.

Player role — Shrouds have significant advantage 
across all stealth intents.

Prior detection — once spotted stealth difficulty 
increases substantially for the rest of that scene.

Equipment — dark clothing, silenced footwear, 
Thorncraft scent suppression preparations.

### Detection Consequence Tiers

Undetected — full stealth maintained.

Suspected — observer is alert but uncertain, 
actively searching.

Spotted — player position known, stealth broken 
for this scene.

Compromised — full alarm, all observers in 
the area notified.

## BUCKET 5 — SKILL USE

### Trigger
Use of role-specific abilities. Runecraft inscription, 
Thorncraft application, Warden survival techniques, 
Shroud operative skills.

### Intent Categories

Inscribe — carve a rune onto a surface, object, 
or body.

Apply — use a Thorncraft preparation or treatment.

Identify — recognize a rune, Blight condition, 
creature type, or operative signature.

Prepare — ready an ability for future use in advance.

Improvise — attempt a skill use without full 
preparation. Higher risk, reduced effectiveness.

Maintain — keep an active ability functioning 
under pressure.

### Preparation Requirement

Runecraft and Thorncraft both require preparation. 
Attempting either without preparation is always 
treated as an Improvise intent — reduced 
effectiveness and higher energy cost.

Wardens and Shrouds do not require preparation 
for core skills but benefit from it. A Warden 
who has observed a creature before fighting it 
resolves combat actions against that creature better.

### Cost and Consequence System

Every skill use costs energy. Complex or powerful 
uses cost more. Using skill abilities when Drained 
is possible but risks pushing into Empty. Using 
skill abilities when Empty is only possible for 
basic applications and always produces a consequence.

### Blight Interaction Rule

Thorncraft used on Blight-corrupted targets is 
more effective but costs more energy and may 
produce secondary effects on the Thornwarden 
depending on the corruption level of the target. 
This must always be reflected in resolution.

## BUCKET 6 — MANIPULATION

### Trigger
Interacting with objects, inventory management, 
environmental interaction, using items, crafting, 
altering the physical environment.

### Intent Categories

Use — activate or apply an item currently in inventory.

Acquire — pick up, purchase, steal, or otherwise 
obtain an item.

Discard — drop, destroy, or give away an item.

Craft — combine or modify items to produce 
something new.

Alter — change the physical environment. Move 
objects, break structures, set traps, create 
or remove obstacles.

Examine — study an object closely to understand 
its properties or origin.

### Resolution Factors

Inventory reality — the player can only use what 
is actually in their inventory. If the item is 
not listed in world state it does not exist for 
the purpose of this action.

Action logic — does the action make physical sense 
given the object, the environment, and the 
character's condition.

Skill requirement — runic items require Runescribe 
knowledge to activate safely. Thorncraft items 
require Thornwarden knowledge to apply correctly. 
Attempting either without the relevant role 
produces unpredictable results.

Time and condition — a Wounded player under 
pressure fumbles fine manipulation tasks.

## BUCKET 7 — NARRATIVE PROGRESSION

### Trigger
Quest updates, faction reputation changes, world 
event consequences, relationship shifts, major 
decisions with lasting impact.

### Intent Categories

Advance Quest — action moves a quest objective forward.

Complete Quest — final objective achieved.

Fail Quest — quest can no longer be completed 
as originally defined.

Shift Reputation — action meaningfully changes how 
a faction or individual NPC views the player.

Trigger World Event — action causes a change in 
the world beyond the immediate scene.

Open Thread — action creates a new unresolved 
situation the world will respond to over time.

### Consequence Permanence Rules

All narrative consequences are permanent unless 
explicitly reversed by a subsequent action of 
equal or greater weight.

Spent resources are gone.
Made enemies remember.
Failed missions have reputational effects.
Dead characters stay dead.
World events triggered cannot be untriggered.

### Reputation Update Rule

Reputation changes must be specific and proportional. 
A single interaction does not dramatically shift 
faction standing. Reputation moves in small 
increments across multiple interactions.

Exceptions — saving a settlement, betraying the Guild, 
killing a faction leader. These cause large immediate 
shifts because they are large immediate actions.

### World State Update Triggers

The following always require a world state update 
after resolution.

Any quest status change.
Any reputation change.
Any NPC disposition change.
Any world flag being set or unset.
Any item entering or leaving inventory.
Any health or energy condition change.
Any new location visited for the first time.

### The World Does Not Wait Rule

Time passes between sessions. The world continues 
without the player. If the player delays acting 
on a time-sensitive situation the world resolves 
it without them. Consequences of inaction are 
as real as consequences of action.

## ROLE CAPABILITIES REFERENCE

This section defines what each role can do and how the 
Resolution Agent should evaluate their capabilities. 
There are no damage numbers. Effectiveness is always 
evaluated logically against the target's condition, 
the situation, and the energy cost of the ability.

---

### RUNECRAFT — Runescribe Capabilities

Runecraft produces effects through inscribed runes. 
All runes fall into one of six categories. Effectiveness 
scales with preparation quality, inscription surface, 
and the Runescribe's energy state.

OFFENSIVE RUNES
Runes that produce damaging or destabilizing effects.
Examples — heat, force, concussive burst, binding.
Prepared offensive runes inscribed in advance on a 
weapon or surface are significantly more effective 
than improvised ones. Heat runes are particularly 
effective against corrupted tissue — Blight-warped 
flesh burns faster than organic flesh. Force runes 
are effective for area disruption. Binding runes 
immobilize rather than damage.
Energy cost — moderate when prepared, high when improvised.

DEFENSIVE RUNES
Runes that absorb, deflect, or reduce incoming harm.
Examples — warding, deflection, hardening, nullification.
Inscribed on skin or armor in advance. A warding rune 
deflects one significant blow before it degrades. 
Hardening runes increase resistance to physical impact. 
Nullification runes are specifically effective against 
other runic effects but have no value against 
non-runic attacks.
Energy cost — low to moderate when prepared, 
high when improvised.

UTILITY RUNES
Runes that produce non-combat environmental effects.
Examples — light, silence, heat generation, 
structural weakening, adhesion.
Highly versatile. A silence rune on a door makes 
it soundless. A structural weakening rune on a 
support beam makes it collapse under load. 
Utility runes are the Runescribe's most flexible 
tool and reward creative preparation.
Energy cost — low when prepared.

DETECTION RUNES
Runes that sense or reveal information.
Examples — proximity warning, Blight detection, 
heat sensing, presence marking.
Useful for scouting and early warning. A proximity 
warning rune on a surface activates when something 
passes near it. Blight detection runes react to 
greywater concentration and Blightseam proximity. 
Detection runes cannot be improvised effectively — 
they require careful preparation to calibrate.
Energy cost — low when prepared, ineffective 
when improvised.

COMMUNICATION RUNES
Runes that transmit information across distance.
Examples — paired message stones, marker runes, 
signal flares.
Rare and expensive to prepare. Paired message 
stones allow short transmissions between two 
pre-inscribed surfaces. Marker runes leave 
identifiable signals for other Runescribes to read.
Energy cost — moderate.

INSCRIPTION RULES
A rune inscribed hastily under pressure is always 
less effective than one prepared in advance. 
Improvised inscription mid-combat is possible but 
treated as an Improvise intent in Bucket 5 — 
reduced effectiveness and higher energy cost.
A Runescribe can maintain a limited number of 
active runes simultaneously. Pushing beyond 
that limit degrades all active runes and 
accelerates energy drain.
Blight-touched surfaces are unstable substrates 
— runes inscribed on them may produce unpredictable 
secondary effects.

### THORNCRAFT — Thornwarden Capabilities

Thorncraft uses Blight-adjacent herbs, corrupted 
materials, and controlled greywater derivatives 
to heal, protect, and in some cases harm. 
Effectiveness scales with preparation quality, 
target condition, and proximity to active Blight.

HEALING PREPARATIONS
Thorncraft treatments that close wounds, stabilize 
condition, and accelerate recovery.
More effective than conventional medicine at every 
condition level. A Wounded character treated with 
Thorncraft recovers faster than one treated 
conventionally. A Critical character can be 
stabilized with Thorncraft when nothing else works.
Against Blight-corrupted injuries specifically — 
Thorncraft is the only effective treatment. 
Conventional medicine does nothing for corruption damage.
Energy cost — moderate. Higher against heavily 
corrupted injuries.
Blight interaction risk — treating severe corruption 
exposes the Thornwarden to trace corruption themselves. 
This must be reflected in resolution for significant cases.

BLIGHT RESISTANCE PREPARATIONS
Pre-journey treatments that slow corruption exposure 
for the whole party.
Applied before entering the Greymark. Reduces the 
rate at which prolonged Greymark exposure affects 
the health track. Does not eliminate Blight risk — 
it extends the window before effects begin.
Energy cost — low to prepare, no cost to apply to others.
Duration — lasts one full journey into the Greymark 
before requiring reapplication.

OFFENSIVE THORNCRAFT
Concentrated corrupted materials weaponized for 
direct application.
Examples — greywater extract applied to a blade, 
corruption accelerant thrown at a target, 
Blightseam residue used as area denial.
Effective against organic targets. Particularly 
effective against non-corrupted creatures and 
humans — corruption accelerants cause rapid 
deterioration of the health track if untreated.
Less effective against already-corrupted creatures — 
they have existing resistance to Blight effects.
Energy cost — high. Offensive Thorncraft is 
always a significant expenditure.
Risk — handling offensive Thorncraft materials 
without proper preparation risks self-exposure.

SUPPRESSION PREPARATIONS
Treatments that mask the player's presence from 
Blight-sensitive creatures.
Greywalkers and other corrupted creatures hunt 
partly by sensing Blight differential — the 
contrast between a clean human and the corrupted 
environment. Thorncraft suppression reduces 
this contrast making the player harder to detect.
Pairs naturally with Bucket 4 Stealth actions 
in the Greymark.
Energy cost — low to prepare.

THORNCRAFT LIMITS
Thorncraft cannot resurrect the dead.
Thorncraft cannot fully reverse advanced corruption 
— it can slow and manage it but not eliminate it 
once established.
A Thornwarden operating at Drained energy applies 
treatments at reduced effectiveness.
Controversial nature — in settlements with kingdom 
presence, visible use of Thorncraft may trigger 
negative social consequences. This must be 
factored into resolution when relevant.

### WARDEN — Core Capabilities

Wardens are trained hunters and survival specialists. 
Their capabilities are not magical but are the product 
of years of field experience in the Greymark.

TRACKING AND SURVIVAL
Wardens read terrain, weather, and creature sign 
better than any other role. In Bucket 3 Exploration 
actions involving wilderness navigation, creature 
tracking, or Greymark survival a Warden resolves 
at higher effectiveness than other roles.
A Warden who has encountered a specific creature 
type before retains that knowledge — subsequent 
encounters with the same type resolve better.

COMBAT PROFICIENCY
Wardens are the most capable role in direct combat. 
Weapon proficiency is broad — blades, bows, 
improvised weapons, hand to hand. Armor use does 
not impede a Warden's movement the way it would 
an untrained character.
Against corrupted creatures specifically — Wardens 
know where to strike. Knowledge of Greywalker 
vein lines, corrupted creature weak points, and 
behavioral patterns translates directly into 
more effective combat resolution.

CREATURE KNOWLEDGE
Wardens can identify corrupted creature types, 
assess their condition track, predict behavior 
archetype, and estimate threat level from 
observation alone. This functions as a Scout 
intent in Bucket 3 before combat begins and 
improves combat resolution when used.

FIELD MEDICINE
Basic non-Thorncraft wound treatment. Effective 
for Scratched and Hurt conditions. Cannot treat 
Wounded or worse effectively without Thorncraft 
support. Cannot treat corruption damage at all.

WARDEN LIMITS
Wardens have no magical capability. Runic items 
can be carried and used at basic level but cannot 
be inscribed. Thorncraft materials cannot be 
prepared or applied safely without Thornwarden 
knowledge.
A Warden's advantages are physical and experiential. 
They degrade with health and energy condition 
the same as any other role.

### SHROUD — Core Capabilities

Shrouds operate in the space between the Guild 
and the criminal underworld. Their capabilities 
center on information, infiltration, and leverage.

INFILTRATION AND STEALTH
Shrouds are the most capable role across all 
Bucket 4 Stealth intents. In low-light, urban, 
or structured environments a Shroud resolves 
stealth actions at significantly higher 
effectiveness than other roles.
Specific advantage — a Shroud can maintain 
stealth under conditions that would compromise 
any other role.

INFORMATION GATHERING
Shrouds know how to find, extract, and verify 
information. In Bucket 2 Social actions involving 
Gather Information a Shroud resolves at higher 
effectiveness. They know which questions to ask, 
who to ask them to, and how to read whether 
the answer is true.
Network access — Shrouds have contacts. In 
settlements with underworld presence a Shroud 
can access information channels unavailable 
to other roles. This must be factored into 
resolution when the player attempts to use 
existing contacts.

LEVERAGE AND DECEPTION
Shrouds are trained in the construction and 
maintenance of false identities, manufactured 
evidence, and social manipulation. Deceive and 
Manipulate intents in Bucket 2 resolve at 
higher effectiveness for Shrouds.
A Shroud caught in a deception faces greater 
consequences than other roles — their reputation 
depends on never being caught and exposure 
damages that reputation severely.

OPERATIVE SKILLS
Lockpicking, trap identification, document 
forgery, coded communication. These are 
treated as prepared skills — a Shroud 
attempting any of these resolves at standard 
effectiveness. Any other role attempting them 
is treated as an Improvise intent.

SHROUD LIMITS
Shrouds are not front-line combatants. In direct 
combat against prepared opponents a Shroud 
resolves at lower effectiveness than a Warden 
of equivalent condition. Their combat advantage 
comes from ambush, misdirection, and ending 
fights before they start — not sustaining them.
Shrouds have no magical or Thorncraft capability.
Their network contacts are assets that can be 
burned — using a contact for something dangerous 
may make that contact unavailable or hostile 
in future interactions.
